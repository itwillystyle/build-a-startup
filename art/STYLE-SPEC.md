# STYLE-SPEC.md: making the world look like one studio made it

6 Oct 2026. Extends `ART.md`, which stays the source of truth for *what* the
look is. This file is about *why the look is not in the game yet* and what
enforces it from here.

---

## The decision

Two ways to kill the "assets dropped in" feeling:

- **handmade** — irregularity, wear, clutter, accident
- **one studio** — a tight palette, one shape language, one committed light,
  one repeated motif

**We are committing to "one studio."** Handmade is authored object by object by
a level artist over months; this world is generated by script at runtime, so
handmade would mean fighting the pipeline with the scarcest resource we have.
Coherence is a rules problem, and rules are enforceable by instrument.

It is also the right answer for the subject. A Silicon Valley campus is
*supposed* to look controlled. The one place mess belongs is the garage — and
the game is about leaving the garage, so the art can carry the progression:
**scruffy and mismatched at the start, immaculate and coherent by the tower.**

---

## The measured gap

`ART.md` is already a good bible. Nothing checks it, so the game has drifted.
All figures measured live, 6 Oct, Dome path at wafer level 43, world settled:

| `ART.md` rule | Reality | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A palette of 9 colours | **191 authored colours** | 21x the palette |
| Every neutral tinted, no pure white or black | **724 untinted greys**, 719 of them the single Roblox default (163,162,165) | colour was never set at all |
| No sharp 90 degree edge over 2 studs | **2,951 sharp blocks (29% of parts)** | the shape language is absent |
| Imported packs re-tinted on entry | **418 pack meshes rendering in the pack's own colours** | the "dropped in" feeling, as a number |
| Hue and saturation match an anchor | **998 parts match none** | real colour drift |
| Neon only on lights, screens, effects | **94 Neon parts untagged** (Clerestory 40, RackLight 12) | no tag existed to hold the rule |
| No built-in Brick/Wood/DiamondPlate/Cobblestone | **0 violations** | **passing** - the bible is followable |

Measured 6 Oct, Dome path at wafer level 43, world settled, stable across two
runs 8 s apart. Frozen in `src/ReplicatedStorage/StyleBaseline.lua`.

Worst single offenders: `RingCar` 201 and `DistCar` 115 untinted (cars are on
ART.md's "never" list), `ParkLeaf` 216 off-palette, `Plot1` 403 sharp blocks,
`RoadKerb` 256 sharp.

### Two numbers in the first draft of this spec were wrong

Written down because the correction is the useful part. The first pass reported
1,962 colours and 5,290 off-palette parts. Both were instrument faults, found
by interrogating the instrument before trusting it:

- **The classifier picked the nearest anchor by hue and only then tested
  saturation.** PaperWhite, WarmConcrete, CaliforniaGold and BrandGold all sit
  within 0.01 of hue 0.11 - one warm family differing only in saturation - so a
  warm off-white was assigned to BrandGold and failed for being 0.68 less
  saturated, while two other anchors would have accepted it. A colour now
  passes if ANY anchor accepts it.
- **On a MeshPart, `Color` is a multiplier over the asset's own vertex colours,
  not a colour.** The per-instance variation pass leaves 4,568 hill-tree parts
  at values like 223,225,227 - a 2% tint, not a grey tree. Reading those as
  colours produced 45% of the off-palette count and 65% of the flat-grey count,
  and counting them as distinct colours measured the variation pass, which is
  deliberate and good, and called it sprawl.

Had either shipped, the baseline would have frozen thousands of phantom faults
and the instrument would have been useless in the direction that matters.

**The read:** this is not an art-talent problem and not a low-poly problem.
Low-poly is a technique thousands of games share; it cannot make anything
unique by itself. The incohesion is that five asset sources each arrived with
their own colour decisions and nothing ever mapped them onto one world.

## 1. Palette: nine hues, unlimited shades

Nine flat colours cannot build a world — which is why the palette was quietly
abandoned. The fix is to make the palette a set of **hue families with free
lightness**, not a set of fixed swatches.

Keep the `ART.md` table as the anchors. Compliance is tested in **HSV**:

- **hue** within 0.02 of an anchor (wrap-aware, about 7 degrees)
- **saturation** within 0.08 of that anchor
- **value** free in 0.15 to 1.00

So `Oak` is not one brown, it is every brown on that hue. The world keeps its
richness and loses its disagreement.

Two absolute rules on top:

- **No neutral below saturation 0.02.** Every grey is tinted toward the brand
  hue. This single rule kills all 730 default-grey parts and both pure
  extremes at once.
- **Full saturation is reserved.** Only the company accent, brand gold, UI
  actions and rare-talent effects may exceed saturation 0.75. That is what
  makes the player's own company pop out of a mid-saturation world.

**Imported packs are re-tinted on entry.** A pack's own colours never reach the
game. This is the permanent tax that buys the look.

---

## 2. The motif: the wafer

A style needs one shape a stranger can point at. Recommended:

> **Everything in this world is a rounded slab or a disc. Nothing has a sharp
> corner.**

The disc is a silicon wafer. It is native to the subject, it is already
structurally present (the Wafers HQ is a ring, the Dome is a dome), it reads
from a 500-stud tower down to a 16 px UI icon, and it contrasts maximally with
what is there now — 2,955 hard-edged boxes. Adopting it *is* the fix for the
corner rule rather than a second project.

Applications: round plinths and planters, disc contact shadows, round tables
and rugs, radiused floor plates, circular lawn beds, roundabouts, disc UI
badges, the collect pad, the HQ ring.

**Alternatives considered:**

- *The chamfer seam* — one cut corner on every object with the company accent
  showing in the cut. Cheaper, applies to boxes without remodelling, but it is
  a detail rather than a silhouette, so it dies at phone distance.
- *The rocket* — already the brand object, but a mascot is not a shape rule.
  It stays the logo and the reward, not the motif.

---

## 3. The light

`ART.md` names the style after a lighting condition and the game is lit bright
and close to neutral, so every asset keeps its own colours. A strongly coloured
key is the cheapest unifier in 3D.

- Golden hour is the authored moment; the 22-minute clock must be tuned so that
  **every minute of it is a designed state**, not an interpolation between two.
- Warm key about (255, 174, 102); cool ambient about (120, 132, 160); low
  ambient so the darks are actually dark. The current frame has almost no value
  below 0.7, which is why restoring shadows changed less than expected.

---

## 4. What gets enforced

A new `SVStyle` module, run the way `SVCheck` is, with five gates:

1. **Palette** — every opaque part passes the HSV test above.
2. **Neutrals** — zero parts below saturation 0.02; zero pure white or black.
3. **Corners** — count of `Part` blocks over 2 studs that are not meshes and
   not tagged `SVStructural` (invisible collision, which nobody sees).
4. **Neon** — only on parts tagged `SVEmissive` by the builder. A tag, not a
   name guess: name-sniffing is why `Clerestory` reads as a light under one
   rule and not another.
5. **Colour sprawl** — distinct colours per system, so a new builder cannot
   quietly introduce 200 shades.

### The ratchet

A rule violated 2,955 times cannot fail the build on day one, and a rule that
only prints a number gets ignored. So the check stores a **baseline file** of
today's counts and fails if any number goes **up**.

Nothing has to be fixed to turn it on. It only guarantees the gap never widens,
and every number becomes a score to drive down. Same move that made `SVCheck`
useful: the instrument first, then the fixes.

## 5. Order of work

| Phase | Work | Why in this order |
|---|---|---|
| **S1** | `SVStyle` + the baseline file, no fixes | **DONE 6 Oct.** Nothing can be verified until it is measured, and the numbers stop getting worse immediately |
| **S2** | Re-tint on entry: one `Palette.map(color)` that every builder and every import passes through | Kills the biggest gap (1,962 shades to 9 hue families) and is mostly one function |
| **S3** | The 730 default-grey parts and the 797 pure extremes | Largest visible win per hour; these are parts nobody ever coloured |
| **S4** | Cars into the palette | 381 parts, explicitly on the "never" list, and they move, so the eye tracks them |
| **S5** | The motif: radiused slabs and discs in `CampusArch`, `CityKit`, grounds | The expensive one; needs Blender work, so it goes after the cheap wins |
| **S6** | The light: commit golden hour, lower the ambient, author the clock | Last, because re-lighting a world whose colours are still wrong means tuning twice |

S1 to S4 need no Blender and no uploads.

### What S1 found that S2 has to decide

**The palette is probably missing two anchors,** and this is a judgement call,
not a measurement:

- **Foliage green.** `CampusLawn` is hue 0.270. Park and hill foliage sits at
  hue 0.36 (62,142,76 / 78,168,92 / 74,132,66). A mown lawn and a tree canopy
  are not the same green in any real palette, so either the foliage moves to
  the lawn hue or the palette gains a `Foliage` anchor.
- **A dark neutral ramp.** `Ink` is one very specific dark blue-grey
  (saturation 0.375). Road and kerb greys (46,50,58 / 64,64,68) share its hue
  but are far less saturated, so they fail on saturation alone.

Widening the tolerance is the wrong fix for both: the sweep shows that going
four times looser (hue 0.08, saturation 0.30) only takes off-palette from 998
to 472, so the remainder is genuine colour disagreement rather than a
threshold artefact. **The tolerances stay at 0.02 / 0.08.**

---

## 6. What this costs

Packs can no longer be dropped in. Every new asset passes the palette map and
the corner rule before it enters the game. That is a permanent tax on asset
acquisition, and it is the actual price of looking unique — which is most of
why so few games pay it.

---

## 7. Not verified

- No part of this has been tested on a phone.
- The motif is a judgement call, not a measurement. It is the one decision here
  that is taste, and it should be seen in a styleframe before S5 is funded.
- That golden hour unifies mismatched assets is standard practice, not
  something measured in this project.
- The HSV tolerances were a guess and are now checked: the S1 sweep kept them
  at 0.02 / 0.08, because loosening them four times over recovers only half the
  faults, which means the rest are real.
- `neonLoose` currently counts "not tagged `SVEmissive`" rather than "Neon on
  something that is not a light". No tag exists yet, so the 94 is an upper
  bound; the number only becomes meaningful once S2 tags the legitimate ones.
